Guide 1 — Support Card Building & Decision Workflow

«Purpose: Provide the AI with a searchable framework for evaluating support cards, constructing support decks, and making support-card investment decisions.

Source basis: Reorganized and condensed from the provided Support Card Evaluation Doc. Card-specific evaluations should be treated as reference information rather than universal rules.

Important: The original source evaluates cards using a particular scenario/meta environment. Do not assume its rankings remain valid after a major scenario/meta change. Use the card database for current card details.»

---

1. SUPPORT CARD DECISION WORKFLOW

When recommending support cards, follow this order.

Step 1 — Identify the Training Goal

Determine:

- Distance
- Strategy
- Required stats
- Important skills
- Scenario
- Available support cards
- Limit Break levels
- Whether the goal is PvP optimization or general progression

Step 2 — Identify the Deck's Main Requirements

Determine:

1. Required stats
2. Required skills
3. Required support-card types
4. Scenario-specific requirements
5. Available Limit Break levels
6. Cross-training requirements

Step 3 — Check Scenario Requirements

Before comparing normal cards:

- Check whether the scenario has a required/specialized Friend or Group card.
- Check scenario-specific mechanics that affect card value.
- Do not blindly use a general card ranking when the scenario changes how cards perform.

Step 4 — Evaluate Candidate Cards

Compare candidates using:

1. Skill compatibility
2. Main-stat training output
3. Cross-training value
4. Limit Break level
5. Specialty rate
6. Friendship bonus
7. Training bonus
8. Motivation bonus
9. Stat bonuses
10. Race bonus
11. Initial bond/stat bonuses
12. Scenario compatibility

Step 5 — Build the Deck

Prefer the cards that solve the build's actual requirements rather than simply selecting the highest numerical card ratings.

Step 6 — Check the Final Deck

Verify:

- Required skills are covered.
- Required stats can realistically be trained.
- Important scenario requirements are covered.
- No card is included solely because of a high rating.
- Limit Break assumptions are realistic.
- The deck does not contain unnecessary duplicate functionality.

2. CORE CARD EVALUATION RULES

Card Strength — Core Rule

There is no single universal way to judge support-card strength.

The source evaluates cards primarily around their ability to produce desirable PvP stats.

A card's practical value comes from a combination of:

- Main-stat training power
- Cross-training value
- Skills
- Specialty rate
- Friendship bonus
- Training bonus
- Motivation bonus
- Race bonus
- Event/initial stats
- Limit Break level
- Scenario compatibility

---

Card Comparison — Important Rule

Similar Scores Are Usually Close

When cards have relatively small score differences, do not automatically treat the higher score as strictly better.

The source specifically notes that cards within roughly 20–40 score should often be considered practically similar.

When scores are close:

«Compare skills and build compatibility before choosing the higher-scoring card.»

---

Skills Can Override Raw Card Score

For PvP builds, a card with slightly lower statistical output may be better if it provides the skills the build actually needs.

Decision Rule

IF card scores are close
THEN compare skills
BEFORE selecting based on score alone.

---

3. SUPPORT CARD STAT PRIORITY

The source's simulation found the following general ordering for bonuses that increase training output:

1. Flat stat bonuses
2. Training bonus / Motivation bonus
3. Friendship bonus
4. Specialty rate
5. Race bonus
6. Other bonuses such as initial stats

Important Details

- Speed bonuses are particularly valuable because they can affect multiple training categories.
- Skill Point bonuses can be extremely valuable.
- Motivation bonus has strong practical value.
- Friendship bonus directly improves friendship training.
- Specialty rate is useful, especially around the initial specialty-rate range, but additional specialty rate has diminishing value.
- Race bonus can become much more important when a relevant breakpoint is reached.

---

4. RACE BONUS BREAKPOINT RULE

Race bonus should not be judged only by its displayed contribution to the general card score.

The source specifically warns that important race-bonus thresholds can significantly change card value.

Decision Rule

IF adding a card's Race Bonus crosses an important breakpoint
THEN increase the practical value of that card.

Do not assume the ranking score already accounts for every breakpoint.

Example From Source

A card that increases Race Bonus enough to cross a major threshold can be substantially better than its normal ranking suggests.

---

5. LIMIT BREAK RULES

Limit Break level is a major part of support-card value.

General Rule

A high-quality SSR at low Limit Break is not automatically better than a fully Limit Broken SR.

The source notes:

- MLB SR cards can outperform low-LB SSR cards.
- Some SSR cards are unusually strong at low Limit Break levels.
- This is the exception rather than the general rule.

AI Decision Rule

Always compare:

Card quality + Limit Break level

rather than:

SSR > SR

---

6. LOW-LB / NON-MLB CARD RULES

When evaluating a card below MLB:

- Check its actual Limit Break effects.
- Do not assume the MLB ranking represents its low-LB performance.
- Some cards are usable at 0–1LB.
- Some cards depend heavily on additional Limit Breaks.
- Some cards have unusual mechanics that make simulation scores less reliable.

Special Cases

Be cautious with cards that have:

- Very low initial bond
- Very low specialty rate
- Unique activation conditions
- Bonuses dependent on specific actions
- Scenario-specific mechanics

The source specifically warns that simulation results can become less representative for unusual cards.

7. SUPPORT CARD TYPE RULES

7.1 SPEED CARDS

General Priority

Speed cards are broadly useful and commonly form the foundation of many builds.

When comparing Speed cards, consider:

- Speed training output
- Skill quality
- Strategy compatibility
- Distance compatibility
- Cross-training value
- Skill Point generation
- Limit Break level

Skill Priority

A Speed card with excellent build-specific skills may be preferable to a slightly stronger statistical Speed card.

Source Examples

The source highlights cards such as:

- Tokai Teio — strong Leader-focused Speed card
- Almond Eye — highly universal Speed card with multiple skill choices
- Admire Groove — Mid-focused Speed card
- Still in Love — well-rounded Speed card with strong skill-point-oriented bonuses
- Rhein Kraft — Mile/frontliner specialist
- Dream Journey — Chaser specialist
- Narita Brian — strong stats but Leader-focused skills
- Smart Falcon — Runner specialist
- Vivlos — highly universal Speed card with strong stat output

These examples are reference descriptions from the source, not a permanent current meta ranking.

---

7.2 STAMINA CARDS

General Rule

Stamina card value depends heavily on the build's distance and training requirements.

For longer-distance builds:

«Prioritize strong direct Stamina output.»

For shorter-distance builds that still use a Stamina card:

«Cross-training value can become more important.»

Decision Rule

IF long-distance build
THEN prioritize reliable Stamina output and relevant recovery/skills.

IF shorter-distance build
THEN consider cross-training value and skill utility more heavily.

Do not use the same Stamina-card ranking for every distance.

---

7.3 POWER CARDS

The source warns that Power-card rankings are unusual.

Power is an important stat, but Power training may be selected partly because the build needs Power rather than because Power cards are the most efficient source of overall stats.

Evaluation Rule

Do not judge Power cards only by raw simulated score.

Consider:

- Power requirement
- Cross-training
- Skills
- Distance/strategy compatibility
- Whether Power can be obtained efficiently from other cards

---

7.4 GUTS CARDS

General Rule

Guts is generally not the stat that should simply be pushed as high as possible.

Therefore:

«Guts cards should often be selected based on their skills and useful bonuses rather than raw Guts output alone.»

The source identifies Urara as a useful beginner/free Guts option, while noting her low starting bond.

Decision Rule

IF Guts card is being considered
THEN prioritize useful skills and overall deck value,
not raw Guts output alone.

---

7.5 WISDOM / INTELLIGENCE CARDS

Wisdom cards should often be evaluated primarily through their skills because the stat differences among strong cards can be relatively small.

Priority

1. Relevant skills
2. Training output
3. Cross-training
4. Specialty rate
5. Other bonuses
6. Limit Break

Source Examples

The source discusses cards including:

8. SKILL-FIRST COMPARISON

When two cards have similar statistical value:

Use This Order

1. Does the card provide a required skill?
2. Does it provide useful alternative skills?
3. Does it match the Uma's strategy?
4. Does it match the race distance?
5. Does it improve skill-point efficiency?
6. Which card has better training output?

Core Principle

«A card should be evaluated as part of the intended build, not in isolation.»

12. REROLL DECISION WORKFLOW

Reroll Priority

For a new player:

1. Look for strong Speed cards.
2. Look for strong Wisdom cards.
3. Consider additional useful SSRs.
4. Consider strong SR cards.
5. Consider duplicate copies of valuable cards because Limit Breaks significantly improve card value.

Core Rule

«A starting account with several useful cards is more valuable than an account containing one impressive but isolated card.»

Banner Rule

Rerolling is more attractive when:

- A strong target card is featured.
- Free-roll periods provide additional attempts.
- Multiple copies can realistically be obtained.

Investment Rule

If a strong featured card is obtained and the player intends to invest in it, the source recommends considering continued investment toward higher Limit Break levels.

13. SSR PICK TICKET WORKFLOW

When using an SSR pick ticket:

Step 1 — Check Existing Copies

Determine whether the ticket can:

- Unlock a useful card
- Increase an existing card's Limit Break
- Complete a high-value card

Step 2 — Prioritize High-LB Value

The source recommends using tickets primarily to bring strong meta cards toward higher Limit Break levels.

Step 3 — Check Skills

If multiple cards have similar strength:

«Choose based on skill usefulness and build compatibility.»

Step 4 — Fill Roster Gaps

The source gives this general investment order:

Speed
↓
Wisdom
↓
Stamina / Guts

This is a general rule, not an absolute requirement.

Step 5 — Consider Waiting

If a major scenario/meta change has just occurred:

«Wait for the meta to settle before making an irreversible decision.»

Step 6 — Saving Is Valid

If there is no clear improvement:

«Save the ticket.»

17. SIMULATION / RANKING INTERPRETATION

The original card rankings come from a simulation rather than a simple list of card abilities.

The simulation considers:

- Possible training combinations
- Training probabilities
- Multiple training phases
- Energy/rest requirements
- Friendship/rainbow opportunities
- Scenario effects
- Race/event stats
- Initial bond
- Race bonus

Cards are compared while other cards in the deck are held relatively constant.

Important Limitation

The source itself warns that this makes rankings less accurate for:

- Weak cards
- Low-LB cards
- Cards with unusual uniques
- Cards with unusual bond/specialty mechanics
- Cards dependent on specific activation conditions

Therefore:

«Treat ranking scores as evidence, not as an absolute answer.»

22. FINAL AI RULE

The AI should not answer:

«"Which support card has the highest score?"»

It should answer:

«"Which available support card best solves the requirements of this specific build, at this player's current Limit Break levels and within this scenario?"»

That is the main purpose of this guide.
